Scoring Rubric

Judging Categories

Each project is scored across 6 criteria totaling 100 points. HP Product Integration carries the highest single weight.

🖥️
25
points · HP Integration
HP Product Integration
Does the project meaningfully use HP technology — such as HP AI Studio, HP hardware, HP Developer APIs, or HP Cloud Services? Integration should be core to the solution, not superficial.
Scoring Levels
5
Exemplary (23–25 pts): HP product is central to the architecture. Deep integration that couldn't work without it.
4
Strong (18–22 pts): HP product meaningfully enhances the solution. Clear value-add beyond a surface-level API call.
3
Moderate (12–17 pts): HP product is used but could be swapped for a generic alternative without changing the core solution.
2
Minimal (6–11 pts): HP product mentioned or used superficially. Not integral to the project.
1
None (0–5 pts): No HP product used. Project may still score well in other categories.
💡
20
points · Innovation
Innovation & Creativity
How original and creative is the idea? Does it approach a problem in a new way, combine technologies unexpectedly, or solve something previously unsolved?
Scoring Levels
5
Exemplary (18–20 pts): Highly novel idea. Judges are surprised. Strong "why hasn't anyone done this before?" reaction.
4
Strong (14–17 pts): Creative twist on an existing concept. Clear originality in approach or implementation.
3
Moderate (9–13 pts): Solid idea with some creative elements. Common problem space but competent execution.
2
Minimal (5–8 pts): Familiar or derivative idea. Limited originality observed.
1
Poor (0–4 pts): Generic or copied concept. No apparent original contribution.
⚙️
20
points · Technical
Technical Complexity
How technically sophisticated is the implementation? Does it demonstrate strong engineering, thoughtful architecture, and effective use of AI/ML, APIs, or hardware within the hackathon timeframe?
Scoring Levels
5
Exemplary (18–20 pts): Impressive technical depth. Non-trivial engineering, smart system design, polished multi-component integration.
4
Strong (14–17 pts): Solid technical execution. Real engineering challenges solved. Not tutorial-level work.
3
Moderate (9–13 pts): Working implementation. Some shortcuts but functional end-to-end.
2
Minimal (5–8 pts): Mostly no-code or low-code. Limited engineering depth.
1
Poor (0–4 pts): Prototype barely works or incomplete. Very limited technical implementation.
🌍
20
points · Impact
Real-World Impact & Feasibility
Does the project solve a meaningful real-world problem? Is the solution practically deployable? How large and underserved is the target audience?
Scoring Levels
5
Exemplary (18–20 pts): Clear, large-scale real-world problem. Solution is deployable today. Compelling market or social case.
4
Strong (14–17 pts): Genuine problem addressed. Path to real-world use is believable with moderate additional work.
3
Moderate (9–13 pts): Valid problem but solution has feasibility questions. Niche or unclear deployment path.
2
Minimal (5–8 pts): Problem not clearly articulated or solution unlikely to scale beyond demo.
1
Poor (0–4 pts): Toy problem or solution with no realistic real-world path.
🎤
10
points · Presentation
Presentation & Demo Quality
How well does the team communicate their project? Is the demo live and compelling? Can they clearly explain the problem, solution, tech stack, and HP integration?
Scoring Levels
5
Exemplary (9–10 pts): Polished, confident, clear. Live demo works flawlessly. Handles Q&A with depth.
4
Strong (7–8 pts): Clear and confident. Demo works. Good storytelling with minor gaps.
3
Moderate (5–6 pts): Understandable but lacks polish. Demo partially works or uses screenshots.
1
Developing (0–4 pts): Unclear pitch. Demo broken or absent. Team struggled to explain the project.
🎨
5
points · Design
Design & User Experience
Is the user interface intuitive, accessible, and visually polished? Does the design enhance usability? Applies to any user-facing component of the project.
Scoring Levels
5
Exemplary (5 pts): Intuitive, polished, and delightful. Clearly designed from the user's perspective.
3
Adequate (3 pts): Functional interface. Usable but basic. Design is an afterthought.
1
Minimal (0–2 pts): Rough or confusing interface. No apparent design consideration.

How It Works

The Judging Process

Submit Your Project

Coding stops at 10 PM on Day 1 (Sept 25). Submit via the registration form before the deadline.

Prepare Your Demo

Day 2 morning (Sept 26): Set up, prepare your 5-minute pitch, and test your HP integration demo live.

Present to Judges

Each team presents to a rotating panel. Explain your HP product usage, problem, solution, and tech stack.

Q&A Round

Judges ask questions — especially around why you chose specific HP tools and how they benefit the solution.

Deliberation

Judges independently score each project across all 6 criteria. Scores are averaged across the panel.

Winners Announced

🏆 Award ceremony at 4 PM on Sept 26. Winners across all categories will be recognized.

💡 Tips for Winning

HP Product Integration is your biggest single opportunity — worth 25 points. Visit the HP booth at the start of Day 1 to learn what APIs and hardware are available, and architect your project around HP tools from the beginning.
